# RENTit — case study

A managed peer-to-peer rental layer for high-value, low-frequency items, starting with cameras and power tools in one dense urban cluster.

**Live site: https://ajinkyaborgaonkar.github.io/rentit/**

---

## The gap

Indians can rent almost anything except the one thing they need for two days. The obstacle is not supply or demand. It is the cost of trusting a stranger: the deposit, the argument about condition, the question of who pays when something comes back broken. That cost is usually higher than the rental fee itself. So people buy a ₹65,000 camera to use it four days a year.

RENTit starts from the premise that the product is trust, not listings. The listing is the easy part.

## What it is

Three things have to be true before a stranger's equipment changes hands:

1. **Verified identity** — the renter is a known person, not a phone number.
2. **Condition proof at handover** — photographs, a grade, both signatures, one record.
3. **A guarantee that pays** — a funded pool, a stated deductible, and a written rulebook for what is and is not covered.

The handover happens at a physical point — a society clubhouse or a partner shop — because a ten-minute meeting removes most of the risk that a courier cannot.

## How it makes money

| Line | Price |
|---|---|
| Commission | 25% of the rental, taken from the owner's side |
| Protection fee | 4% of the rental, paid by the renter, minimum ₹49 |
| Cover | Up to ₹50,000 per item, ₹2,500 deductible |
| Owner payout | Within 48 hours of a signed return |
| Pickup | Self-pickup by default; delivery ₹149 each way, free above ₹2,500 |

The protection fee is not an add-on. It funds the guarantee, and the guarantee is what makes the first rental possible.

## The pilot

Ninety days, one cluster: about 3,000 households inside a 3 km radius, two categories, one hub.

| | Target |
|---|---|
| Lenders recruited | 25 |
| Listings live | 60 |
| Orders | 100 |
| Budget | ₹6–8 lakh |
| App | None in phase 1 — landing page, WhatsApp, spreadsheet |

The pilot is judged on four numbers, agreed before it starts:

- Match rate ≥ 60% — most searches return something real
- Damage and loss ≤ 4% of GMV
- 25% of renters repeat within six months
- Contribution ≥ ₹250 per order, self-pickup

If those fail, the plan says stop or change category — not scale.

## What the site is

Three pages, written as a product rather than a pitch:

- **Home** — what it is, what it costs, the record that makes it work
- **How it works** — the protocol before, during and after handover, the grading scale, the cover matrix, the 48-hour claim clock
- **For owners** — the maths, with a calculator that subtracts upkeep and delivery so the number is honest

## Built with

Static HTML, CSS and one small JavaScript file. No framework, no build step, no analytics, no third-party requests. Fonts are self-hosted, so the site works offline and from a local file.

Design direction: warm paper ground, ink panels, one signal orange per screen, blueprint accents — a deliberate move away from the dark-gradient look most rental startups use.

---

The site is a storefront for a pilot that has not launched. Nothing is listed for rent, and the copy says so rather than implying traction.
